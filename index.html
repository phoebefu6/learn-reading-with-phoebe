The six sessions 📚 · from the speed myth to the technical paper

The running case is Mina, a constructed analyst at Harbourline, an invented shipping company. By Friday she must recommend one of two warehouse-software vendors, and the pile is a 60-page vendor report, two research papers, the vendor's API documentation and a dashboard. Her first move was a speed-reading app. Mina, Harbourline and every number about them are invented for teaching, and every page says so.

⏱️ Session 1 · foundations

What reading speed really measures

Adults read non-fiction at about 238 words per minute, not 300. Why words, not eyes, set the pace, and what speed-reading courses, RSVP apps and the war on inner speech trade away.

What is honest here 🧾

Reading advice travels with confident numbers and famous techniques. Each claim on these pages carries its status.

ClaimStatus
Your words per minute, comprehension and effective rate on the benchReally measured in your browser: a real clock on three public-domain passages and five questions each. Results stay in this browser
What rereading tonight or spaced retrieval would leave you with in 30 daysModelled by a forgetting model printed on the widget, with illustrative settings; only the ordering of schedules is claimed
"The average reader reads 300 words per minute"A repeated number. A 190-study meta-analysis found 238 for silent reading of non-fiction, with most adults between 175 and 300. Session 1
"Speed reading triples your speed with full comprehension"; "stop subvocalising"Not supported. The tested gains came with lower comprehension, and suppressing inner speech hurts on harder texts. Session 1 teaches both sides
"Match teaching to your learning style"No adequate evidence base for the version that matters: the crossover result the hypothesis needs was not found. Session 3
